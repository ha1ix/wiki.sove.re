# Nomadics — General Nomad Notes

*Cross-country advice compiled from community digests.*

## Settling Down & Lifestyle Patterns

Common pattern: cycling between settling (3–12 months in one place) and moving. Kids and community cited as the primary anchors that shift people toward longer stays. The "perpetual itch" — difficulty staying put even when settled — is widely reported. Bangkok is a common choice for those transitioning from full nomad to semi-settled, given its infrastructure and cost of living.

### Worldschooling

Families traveling with children while homeschooling/unschooling on the road. Cape Town and Bansko mentioned as recurring hubs with worldschooling community presence.

## Connectivity & SIMs

eSIM recommendations from the community (as of April 2026): Saily Ultra eSIM for data, paired with a Tello US number for maintaining a US phone number abroad (activatable outside the US using a VPN). Roamless Flex also well-regarded. Note: Tello activation requirements have changed — verify current process before relying on it.

## Gear Insurance

Community consensus leans toward self-insuring laptops and gear via savings and cloud backups rather than traditional insurance policies. Premiums, deductibles, and claims hassle generally outweigh the coverage for nomads.

## Traveling Long-Term with a Partner

Reported strategies from the community:

- **Avoid double rent.** Keeping a home apartment while abroad long-term is generally advised against — the sunk cost is significant.
- **Hybrid setup.** One workable model: maintain a long-term rental in a low-cost hub (Kuala Lumpur cited as an example), store belongings with family when away, and negotiate direct landlord deals for better monthly rates instead of going through platforms.

## Health Insurance

Genki frequently recommended as nomad health insurance. SafetyWing also used — reimbursement process reported as positive by some users. Key caveats for any nomad insurance: expect upfront out-of-pocket payments (reimbursement model, not direct billing), watch for exclusions, and read full policy terms before relying on coverage. Fine print matters more than brand reputation.

## US LLCs for Nomads

### Wyoming vs Delaware

Community consensus (as of July 2026) leans Wyoming over Delaware for a solo-member LLC — lower formation and annual costs, and stronger member-privacy defaults (member names not on public record). Delaware's advantages skew toward multi-member structures and companies planning to raise venture capital.

### EIN & Banking

An EIN is obtainable by non-US residents without SSN/ITIN (Form SS-4 by fax/mail). The friction point is banking, not the LLC itself.

- **Traditional US banks** effectively require a US proof of address and SSN or ITIN. Not practical for most non-resident nomads. Side benefit if you do obtain an ITIN: it also builds US credit history over time, which can matter later for card/loan access.
- **Fintech (Mercury, Wise, etc.)** is the usual path. Mercury reportedly accepts non-US owners with an EIN and proof of address in the owner's country of residence (UAE, Italy, etc. reported working), though final approval is discretionary — no guaranteed acceptance regardless of paperwork.
- **US phone number** for verification: Tello eSIMs are a common workaround for maintaining a US number abroad. Activation-from-abroad requirements have shifted more than once — verify current process before relying on it.

### Tax Residency Risk

Owning a US LLC does not shield you from your country of tax residence. A US LLC is a pass-through entity for US federal tax purposes, so profits flow to the owner personally under the rules of wherever the owner is tax-resident.

For any nomad crossing a country's tax-residency threshold (typically 183 days, but varies), assume that country will treat US LLC income as personal income unless the company has genuine substance elsewhere — real office, real staff, real decisions made abroad. Paper substance does not count.

## Spain — Tax Residency for LLC Owners

Reported community understanding (July 2026): Spanish tax residents with a US LLC owe Spanish tax on worldwide income from the LLC unless the company has real substance outside Spain. Paper offshore structures do not survive scrutiny.

Practical threshold: for most freelancers, registering locally in Spain (autónomo or SL) is simpler and often cheaper in effective tax than maintaining a US LLC — until revenue reaches roughly **$200–300k/year**, at which point the analysis changes and the LLC + offshore substance route may pay off. Below that, the compliance overhead and Spanish tax exposure usually outweigh the LLC's benefits.

Not tax advice — verify with a Spanish gestor before restructuring.
