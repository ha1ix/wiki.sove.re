# Brazil — Nomad Notes

*Compiled from community digests.*

## Payments & Banking

### PIX Without a CPF

PIX payments do **not** require a CPF to send or receive — the CPF is only strictly required when opening a Brazilian bank account. Nomads without a CPF can transact locally through wrapper apps.

Community-recommended payment apps that route around the CPF requirement:

- **Peanut** (Peanut Pix) — uses USDC/crypto rails under the hood, funded via credit card. Currently invite-based. Reported as reliable when used from outside Brazil.
- **Yodl** — alternative to Peanut Pix. Sometimes offers better FX rates than Peanut on larger payments.
- **Truther Wallet** — mentioned as an alternative.
- **Meru** — mentioned as an alternative.

Both Peanut and Yodl reportedly work in Brazil, Argentina, and parts of Asia.

Getting a CPF online is possible but not necessary if the goal is just to pay for things day-to-day.

### Comparing PIX ↔ Crypto/FX Rates

**comparapix.ar** — community-shared comparison site for checking live PIX-to-crypto and FX rates across providers before sending. Useful for picking the cheaper rail on any given day.

### Argentina ↔ Brazil Cross-Border

**Belo** — cited as another option for moving money between Argentina and Brazil. Residency requirements were an open question in the community; verify before relying on it if you're not a resident of either country.

## Rio de Janeiro

### Nightlife

- **Lapa** — recommended cluster of clubs for going-out nights.
- **Pedra do Sal** — samba-scene area worth asking about locally.

### Day Trips

Reachable from Rio for weekend or day escapes: **Búzios**, **Peró**, and **Ilha Grande**.

## Paraná

### Healthcare

For private hospital care in smaller Paraná cities, **Unimed** is the community-recommended network — specifically the Unimed hospitals in **Maringá** and **Londrina**. **Curitiba** is the larger option with better connections, but Maringá and Londrina are viable if you want a smaller city.

## Florianópolis (Floripa)

### Climate & Safety

Subtropical climate — late September reported as rainy and humid. Generally safe for walking at night, with the exception of some parts of **Centro**. Internet is decent island-wide, most people make an effort to speak English, and safety is among the best in Brazil when common sense applies. Heavy traffic in summer is a real friction point.

### Neighborhoods

- **South and east sides (nature-oriented):** Campeche, Armação, Lagoa da Conceição.
- **North (upscale):** Jurere.
- **Ingleses:** large Argentine community.
- **Centro:** has sketchy pockets; avoid walking there at odd hours.

A car is effectively required if you want to reach the beach towns regularly — without one, the island shrinks fast and some find it boring after one or two weeks. If that's your experience, alternatives for the same time of year include Buenos Aires, Montevideo, or the coast south of Salvador.

### Work Scene

Active tech and innovation project-management community. Reported as a viable base for ecommerce and digital marketing work, with in-person peers around.

### IPE City
