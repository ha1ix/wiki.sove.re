# Thailand — Nomad Notes

*Compiled from community digests.*

## Koh Tao

### Diving

Recommended dive operators (as of March 2026): Hydronaughts, Pimp My Dive, Divers Boat, Goodtime, New Haven (Chalok area). March is the start of dive season.

Koh Phangan also has dive spots but less detailed recommendations available.

## Koh Phangan

### Healthcare

Hospital exists on the island but quality reports are mixed. For anything serious, Ko Samui (short ferry) has better facilities. See Ko Samui healthcare section.

### Neighborhoods

Northwest side (Haad Yao, Chalok Lam) regarded as the chill side of the island — better for people wanting quiet beaches and gyms over party scene.

### Notes

Mixed reports on atmosphere. Some friction between locals and tourists reported (scooter near-misses, rudeness). Beaches and nature are the main draw; town areas less so. Best visited with low expectations for nightlife/social scene.

## Chiang Mai

### Air Quality & Burning Season

Burning season runs roughly February–April. As of mid-March 2026: AQI hits red levels in mornings, but conditions varied (some cloudy/rainy days keeping it manageable). Rain has been reported to significantly improve air quality during this period. Air purifiers available in some coworking spaces and hotels — worth confirming before booking. Nomad presence still active during this period despite the air quality.

As of early April 2026: air quality continuing to worsen through the season. Some nomads staying through regardless, waiting for Songkran.

### Neighborhoods

Nimman: popular but noted as noisy — not the quiet retreat some expect.

### Songkran (Thai New Year, mid-April)

Expect chaos. Practical tips: use Grab cars (not scooters), carry dry bags for electronics and documents.

## Pattaya

### Seasonality

Rainy season runs approximately April–November. Rain typically comes in short, intense episodes rather than all-day downpours. Noted as having child-friendly services and infrastructure for families.

## Ko Samui

### Notes

Underrated relative to its Nomad List ranking (as of March 2026). Good beaches, reasonable cost of living, active nightlife. Some power reliability issues reported. Hospital quality on the island is considered decent and significantly cheaper than US equivalents.

### Healthcare

Hospital options across the Gulf islands vary. Ko Samui has the most established facilities; Koh Phangan and Ko Lanta have hospitals but with mixed reports on quality. All considerably cheaper than US healthcare. Worth checking specific hospital reviews before relying on island-based care for anything serious.

## Bangkok

### Overview

Frequently recommended as the easiest affordable entry point for digital nomad beginners. Infrastructure, cost of living, and existing nomad community lower the barrier to entry. Some long-term nomads have settled in Bangkok specifically for routine and stability after years of moving. Pollution and sleaze noted as persistent downsides.

### Neighborhoods

Sukhumvit 11 reportedly shifting toward a "Little India" character (as of April 2026).

For quiet areas comparable to Chiang Mai's Nimman: BTS-adjacent neighborhoods recommended — Nana, Thong Lo, On Nut, Udom Suk. Talat Noi/Songwat (old town riverside area) and Ari also suggested as quieter alternatives with character.

### Flooding (October 2026)

During the September–October 2026 flooding, Sukhumvit was largely spared while other districts remained affected. Consistent with its reputation as one of the more flood-resilient central areas — a factor worth weighing for rainy-season (May–October) stays.

### Food

Healthy eating: Tom Kha Gai and Tom Yum widely available as lower-oil Thai options. Low-oil delivery options exist on Grab but require searching. Finding no-plastic, genuinely low-oil delivery remains difficult.

### Practical

DMK (Don Mueang) airport: night markets located near airport-area hotels — useful for late arrivals or early departures.

Coworking and cafe scene active but no specific venues named in this digest cycle.

### Transit

Bangkok–Surat Thani train route: a practical 12-day itinerary with stops at Sam Roi Yot, Koh Tao, and Koh Phangan before reaching Surat Thani. A good way to see the Gulf coast without flying.

## Chiang Mai / Pai

### Driving

International Driving Permit (IDP) is essential for renting cars — without one, insurance is void and fines apply. For scooters/bikes, enforcement is more lenient and varies by area, though having an IDP is still advisable. Physical copy required (digital not accepted). IDP can be shipped from home country via postal service if not obtained before departure.

In tourist-heavy areas (Phuket especially), police checkpoints targeting foreigners on scooters are common — expect bribes if riding without proper documentation.

### Thai Driver's License — Updated Requirements (July 2026)

Requirements have tightened. TM30 alone is no longer sufficient. In Krabi (and reportedly other provinces), a residency certificate is now mandatory, alongside e-Learning completion, medical certificates, and a DLT (Department of Land Transport) appointment.

Workarounds and notes reported by the community:
- Honda in Chiang Mai runs English-language motorbike courses that issue a 2-year temporary license — a practical shortcut for short-stay nomads.
- English-friendly driving schools are hard to find in general; a driving instructor can help navigate the paperwork during a short stay.
- Phuket has been mentioned as an easier province for foreigners to complete the process.
- Applicants without a home-country license typically need embassy paperwork.
- Thai language ability is not strictly required — many expats have obtained licenses without it. If one province/school is difficult, try another.

## Phuket

### Sports & Recreation

Tennis academies available for intermediate-level adult players. Kali academy mentioned. Long-stay hotel/villa options near academies. Bangkok also has options (Alpha academy noted for older players).

## Visa & Tax

Free 30-day visa extensions have been reported for travelers affected by Middle East war-related flight cancellations (as of early 2026). Confirm current policy with immigration before relying on this.

### Visa Runs & Extensions (April 2026)

Stricter enforcement at Bangkok airport against short (1–2 day) visa runs. Extensions now capped at 2 per year: 30 days on the first, 7 days on the second. Land border entries are ineligible for extensions. Annual stay day caps being applied and scrutiny increasing after repeated runs. Rejections reported at Nonthaburi immigration office.

### DTV (Digital Traveler Visa)

Generally smooth after 180 days. Occasional requests for onward tickets — airport-dependent; having one ready (or using TDAC) advisable. For remote workers: no Thai employer registration needed if employer is offshore, but tax residency risks apply if staying 180+ days in a calendar year.

For short trips (roughly a month), DTV is overkill — tourist visa or visa-free entry is the community-recommended default. DTV only makes sense when planning to stay long enough that visa renewals become the pain point.

### Banking (August 2026)

Wise has been reassigning Thai user addresses, and DTV holders have reported losing Thai bank accounts as a knock-on effect. Community workaround: hold Revolut/Wise accounts opened via Georgia rather than Thailand to avoid the address churn.

Applications go through thaievisa.go.th. Success rates vary noticeably by embassy — applying from a country where the local embassy has a track record of approvals matters more than the paperwork itself. Reports as of July 2026 from Spain and Vietnam among others.

### Tax for Long-Stay Remote Workers (July 2026)

For DTV holders and other long-stayers crossing the 180-day threshold in a calendar year, tax residency risk is real on paper but enforcement is reportedly minimal. Community understanding:

- Only income actually *remitted into Thailand* is subject to declaration. Money kept offshore is not treated as Thai income in practice.
- Many long-stay visitors skip filing entirely with no reported consequences. This is not legal advice — the risk exists, it just isn't being enforced consistently.
- Verify against a Thai tax professional before making decisions with real money on the line.

### LTR vs. Elite Visa

The Long-Term Resident (LTR) visa's passive-income and investment thresholds are high enough that most nomads don't qualify. For those who want multi-year stability without meeting LTR criteria, the Elite Visa is generally cheaper over a long horizon, even accounting for its upfront fee.

### DTV Tax — Remittance Rule

Staying 180+ days in a calendar year on a DTV triggers Thai tax residency. Practical community reading: **only funds actually brought into Thailand need to be declared** — income earned abroad and kept abroad is not treated as Thai-source income under the remittance interpretation. Long-stay visitors reportedly skip filing with little enforcement to date, but this is not a legal position, and rules/enforcement can shift. Verify with a Thai tax advisor before relying on it.

### LTR vs Elite Visa

Community view: the LTR's passive-income and investment thresholds are high enough that the **Elite Visa is often the cheaper long-term option** for nomads who don't already meet LTR wealth criteria. Compare total cost over the intended stay rather than sticker price.
